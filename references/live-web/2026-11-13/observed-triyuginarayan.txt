Triyuginarayan Temple, Uttarakhand
Triyuginarayan Temple, Uttarakhand – The Sacred Wedding Place of Shiv-Parvati

Across India, few temples are as closely connected to the divine love of Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati as Triyuginarayan Temple. Located in Rudraprayag district of Uttarakhand, in the Himalayan hills on the way to Kedarnath, it is believed to be the sacred place where their divine wedding took place.

According to tradition, Goddess Parvati, daughter of King Himavan, performed years of intense tapasya (penance) to win Lord Shiva's heart. Her unwavering devotion finally brought them together in marriage. Lord Vishnu is believed to have stood as Parvati's brother, while Lord Brahma performed the wedding rituals as the priest.

The most remarkable part of this sacred place is its Akhand Dhuni – the eternal wedding fire. The fire that is believed to have witnessed the divine wedding is said to have been burning since Treta Yuga. Devotees consider its sacred ash a blessing and a symbol of a love that stands beyond time.

The name Triyuginarayan brings together Tri (three), Yugi (Yugas or eras) and Narayan (Lord Vishnu). The temple's traditions are deeply connected with the three Yugas and the eternal sacred fire.

Near the temple are holy water tanks called kunds and the Brahma Shila, the stone where the divine wedding is believed to have taken place. Even today, couples travel to this sacred temple to marry at the very place where Shiva and Parvati are believed to have taken their wedding vows.

Monday: The Day of Lord Shiva

Monday, or Somvar, is dedicated to Lord Shiva. It is also associated with the Moon (Chandra), which rests on Shiva's head and represents calmness, emotional balance and peace of mind.

Worshipping Lord Shiva on Mondays is considered especially auspicious. Devotees offer prayers and observe fasts to seek his blessings for love, marriage and family happiness.

Offering Lal Chunri, Gangajal, Mauli and Akhand Dhuni Ahuti at Triyuginarayan Temple every Monday is a way to seek:
The divine blessings of Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati.
Strength, trust and understanding in relationships.
Relief from obstacles and delays in marriage.
Emotional peace, patience and stability.
Love, happiness and harmony within the family.
The Sacred Tradition of 16 Somvar

The Solah Somvar Vrat is a sixteen-Monday tradition dedicated to Lord Shiva. Devotees observe it with prayers for finding a life partner, overcoming marriage obstacles, strengthening relationships and attaining lasting love and marital happiness.

Panchakshari Mantra

The Two Sacred Offerings: Lal Chunri and Gangajal

Lal Chunri: Red is traditionally associated with love, devotion, prosperity and auspicious beginnings. Offering a Lal Chunri to Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati symbolises prayers for a strong, loving and lasting bond. Devotees offer it seeking blessings for marital bliss, relationship harmony, commitment and happiness in the family.

Gangajal (Holy Ganga Water): Lord Shiva is closely associated with the sacred River Ganga, which flows from his matted locks. Gangajal is considered highly purifying in Hindu worship. Offering it to Mahadev symbolises purity, devotion and the removal of negativity.

Together, Lal Chunri, Gangajal, Mauli and Akhand Dhuni Ahuti are among the most cherished offerings to Lord Shiva, made with prayers for love, peace, prosperity and lasting happiness.
