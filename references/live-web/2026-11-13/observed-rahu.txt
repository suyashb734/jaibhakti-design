About the mandir
Rahu Paithani, Uttarakhand

Across India, temples dedicated specifically to Rahu Dev are exceptionally rare. Rahu Paithani Temple in Uttarakhand is one such sacred shrine, making it a significant destination for devotees seeking Rahu Dosh Shanti.

According to the tradition associated with the Kedar Khanda of the Skanda Purana, Rahu Dev performed intense penance (tapasya) to Lord Shiva here, and Mahadev is believed to have blessed this sacred land. The temple is especially significant for its association with both Shiva and Rahu, including the worship of a Shivling and the revered Rahu Shila. Devotees seek Lord Shiva's grace to pacify Rahu's influence and pray for relief from planetary afflictions.

The region was traditionally known as Rathkshetra, while Paithani is believed to derive its name from Paithinasi, the gotra associated with Rahu Dev. Local traditions also associate the temple with Adi Shankaracharya and the Pandavas, adding to its ancient spiritual significance.

Why Rahu Dosha Needs Attention

In Vedic astrology, Rahu is a shadow planet associated with ambition, desires, illusion and karmic influences. Rahu Mahadasha lasts 18 years and its unfavourable influence is traditionally believed to cause:

Career and Financial Setbacks: Unexpected losses, unstable income and repeated hurdles.
Mental Confusion and Fear: Overthinking, restlessness and difficulty finding clarity.
Relationship Disturbances: Misunderstandings, mistrust and family conflicts.
Sudden Obstacles: Unexpected disruptions and recurring struggles despite continuous efforts.
Saturday: An Auspicious Day for Rahu Dosh Shanti

Saturday is dedicated to Shani Dev, who represents karma, discipline and justice. Rahu and Shani are traditionally associated with karmic influences, delays and obstacles. Therefore, Saturday is considered an auspicious day for seeking planetary peace through prayer and charity.

Offering Neel Vastra and Til at Rahu Paithani Temple on Saturdays is believed to help devotees seek:

Relief from Rahu-Shani-related obstacles and delays.
Balance amid difficult karmic influences.
Peace, protection and stability for the family.
Blessings during Rahu Mahadasha and Rahu Antardasha.

The Two Sacred Offerings: Neel Vastra and Til

Neel Vastra (Blue Cloth): Traditionally associated with Rahu and Shani, offering blue cloth symbolises surrendering one's worries and seeking protection, stability and relief from confusion and obstacles.

Til (Black Sesame): An important offering in Hindu rituals and daan, black sesame is traditionally associated with relief from karmic burdens, planetary afflictions and ancestral concerns. Offering Til is believed to invite peace and spiritual balance.

Together, Neel Vastra and Til make an auspicious Saturday offering to Rahu Dev, as devotees seek Rahu Dosh Shanti and the grace of Lord Shiva.
